The four bars are live on the screen: how much that stage is turning the music down at this moment, 0 to 12 dB (cyan under 3 dB, amber from 3 dB, red from 8 dB). The sound with every menu at its first entry and MATCH off is exactly the sound you had before.

AUTO "Full band" (the original)

One gain for the whole mix. Each kick pushes the level up, the leveler turns the whole mix down for a moment, and lets go over 0.2 s.

Bass (kick)VoiceHi-hatsLeveler gain

What you hear: the voice and the hi-hats dip with every kick and swell back. Fast and light it reads as "glue"; deep and slow it reads as pumping or "squashed".

AUTO "Bass only" (new)

The mix is split at 150 Hz. Only what is below is levelled; the voice and the hi-hats never pass through the leveler at all.

Bass (kick)VoiceHi-hatsLeveler gain (bass band)

What you hear: the kick is held in check, the voice and the hats stay exactly steady. The cost: the kick can sound a little less tied to the rest. "Slow release" keeps "Full band" but lets go over 0.6 s, so it breathes less.

Loudness and the Bass slider; MATCH

Adding bass adds loudness. Without MATCH the only cure is Master Volume by hand; with MATCH on, the trim follows the music over a few seconds.

MATCH off (as today)MATCH on

What you hear: with MATCH on, more Bass sounds like a change of tone, not a jump in volume, and the limiter has less to catch. The shape is the point; the dB figures depend on the track.

Lines are drawn thin on purpose; the dashed gain line is the leveler's own control signal. Colours: blue, orange and aqua steps from a colour-blind-checked set; the bars on the screen use the theme colour, amber and red with the figure beside them, never colour alone.
